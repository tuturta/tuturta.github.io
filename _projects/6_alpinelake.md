As **Vice-President for Science (Science Coordinator)**, I led the scientific direction and coordination of the *Alpine Lake 2024 Expedition*, a 10-day interdisciplinary research mission uniting more than 20 EPFL students.  
Our goal was to study the effects of climate change on the planktonic ecosystems and sediments of Lake Geneva, while testing new open-source scientific instruments and low-tech environmental monitoring tools.

### 🔬 Scientific Focus

The expedition investigated how nutrient dynamics, temperature gradients, and pollutant concentrations (PFAS) influence plankton diversity and distribution in alpine freshwater systems.

**Main activities:**
- **Plankton sampling and cultivation** for microscopic structural analysis in collaboration with the *Dudin Lab (EPFL)*.  
- **Chemical and pollutant assays** with the *CEL Lab (EPFL)* to assess microcontaminant accumulation.  
- **Environmental profiling** using the *SenseLab’s CTD sensor*, measuring conductivity, temperature, depth, pH, and dissolved oxygen.  
- **Automated plankton imaging and classification** with an open-source **Planktoscope**, co-developed with the *LGB Lab (EPFL)*.  

This work contributed to developing **accessible, low-cost tools for citizen and academic oceanography**, while generating valuable data for the upcoming *Arvor 2024 marine expedition* in Brittany.

### ⚙️ Low-Tech & Open Science

In parallel, the expedition served as a testbed for low-tech prototypes, including:  
- A **solar oven** and **thermal cooking box (*marmite norvégienne*)** to reduce gas consumption on board.  
- A **Secchi disk** for qualitative assessment of water transparency and photosynthetic activity.  

All instruments and documentation were shared under open-science principles, promoting reproducibility, accessibility, and community collaboration in environmental research.
