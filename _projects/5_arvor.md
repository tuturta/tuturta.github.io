As **Vice-President for Science**, I oversaw the scientific coordination, mission design, and data-driven direction of the *Arvor 2024 Expedition*.  
My role involved defining research objectives with EPFL laboratories, supervising onboard data collection, ensuring experimental reproducibility, and integrating results across biology, chemistry, and environmental sensing.  
I also mentored the student research teams, developed new open-source measurement protocols, and ensured that every scientific activity aligned with Sailowtech’s values of low-tech innovation, open science, and environmental awareness.

The *Arvor Expedition* was Sailowtech’s third major scientific journey, following *Atlantea (2023)* and *Alpine Lake (2024)*.  
From July 18 to August 11 2024, our 18-member EPFL crew sailed aboard the *Mauritius*, a traditional schooner operated by the Fondation Pacifique, navigating between **Brittany, the Scilly Isles, Cardiff, and Skomer Island (Wales)**.  

Our goals were to:
- Study the **impact of climate change on planktonic ecosystems**.  
- Quantify **micro- and nanoplastics** in coastal waters.  
- Test **low-cost, open-source scientific instruments** designed by students.

- **Plankton Ecology & Microscopy**: sampling and classification of phytoplankton and zooplankton communities, studying their diversity, abundance, and relation to physical-chemical gradients (temperature, salinity, pH, oxygen).
- **Microplastic Detection (LNET & CEL, EPFL)**: development of a low-cost Raman spectroscopy setup for in-situ identification of microplastics.  
- **CTD Environmental Profiling (SenseLab, EPFL)**: deployment of a **custom modular CTD probe** measuring: Conductivity, Temperature, Depth, pH, and Dissolved Oxygen.  Data provided essential context for biological and chemical measurements.
- **CO₂ Degassing Chamber (SenseLab, EPFL)**: prototype of a CO₂ extraction cell for dissolved gas monitoring, designed to evaluate ocean acidification in nearshore waters.
- **Planktoscope Imaging (LGB, EPFL)**: Testing of an open-source imaging microscope for quantitative plankton observation, enabling high-resolution imagery and AI-assisted classification.

### ⚙️ Low-Tech & Sustainability

Several student-built low-tech prototypes were tested onboard:
- Solar tube oven and thermal cooking box (*marmite norvégienne*) to reduce fuel use.  
- Pedal-powered system for laundry, blending, and energy generation.  
- Solar desalination prototype for freshwater production.  
- Secchi disk for transparency and euphotic depth measurement.  

All designs were created from recycled materials and openly documented to promote reproducibility and accessible science.
